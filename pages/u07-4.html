The trailing dot matters. In a zone file mail.example.com. is a complete name. Without the final dot BIND adds the zone name, so mail.example.com becomes mail.example.com.example.com.

When to use each record

RecordUse it for
A / AAAAPoint a name at an IPv4 / IPv6 address
CNAMEAlias one name to another (do not place CNAMEs where other record types must exist, such as on the zone apex in many setups)
MXMail servers; lower preference number = higher priority
NSDelegate or publish the name servers for a zone
PTRReverse DNS (IP → name); needed for many mail servers
TXTSPF, DKIM, domain proof for certificates or Google/Microsoft
SOAZone identity and timers; one per zone; serial must increase on edits

The trailing-dot rule

In zone files, a name ending with a dot is absolute (www.school.lk.). Without the dot, the zone origin is appended. Writing www.school.lk inside zone school.lk can accidentally become www.school.lk.school.lk.

Common mistake: forgetting to bump the SOA serial after an edit. Secondaries may never fetch the new data.

Remember

  • A/AAAA for hosts, MX for mail, NS for delegation, PTR for reverse.
  • Absolute names end with a dot in zone files.

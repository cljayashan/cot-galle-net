Collision domain vs broadcast domain. A collision domain is the area where frames can collide (every hub port shares one; each switch port has its own). A broadcast domain is the area reached by a broadcast frame (a whole switch or VLAN; a router stops broadcasts).

How a switch learns. A switch reads the source MAC of every frame and records it with the port in its MAC address table. When a frame arrives, it looks up the destination MAC: if known, it sends out that port only (forwarding); if unknown or broadcast, it floods all other ports.

How a router decides. A router looks at the destination IP of the packet, finds the best match in its routing table, and sends the packet to the next hop. The route used when nothing else matches is the default route (0.0.0.0/0).

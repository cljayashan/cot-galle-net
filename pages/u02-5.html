2.5The Internet, intranet and extranet

  • Internet: the global network of networks, connected by ISPs and using TCP/IP.
  • Intranet: a private network of an organisation, using Internet technologies, accessible only to its members.
  • Extranet: an intranet partly opened to outside partners (suppliers, customers) under controlled access.

The Internet in more detail

The Internet is not owned by any one organisation. It is made of thousands of independent networks (ISPs, universities, companies and cloud providers) that agree to exchange traffic, all using the TCP/IP protocols (see 3.4.2). Large networks connect at Internet exchange points (IXPs), and undersea fibre cables join the continents. When you visit a website, your traffic usually passes through several of these networks.

The World Wide Web is not the same as the Internet. The Web is one service that runs on the Internet (web pages over HTTP and HTTPS). Email, online games, video calls and file transfer are other services that use the same Internet.

Intranet

An intranet uses the same technologies as the Internet (web servers, browsers, email, TCP/IP), but only the organisation's own staff can use it. Typical content: staff news, policies and forms, the HR leave system, internal phone lists and project sites. Staff reach it from inside the office, or from home through a VPN (see Unit 6). Because it is private, it can hold information that must not be public.

Extranet

An extranet opens selected parts of the intranet to trusted outsiders, after they log in. For example:

  • a supermarket lets its suppliers see stock levels, so that they can deliver before shelves are empty;
  • a university lets partner colleges enter student marks;
  • a manufacturer lets dealers place orders and track deliveries.

Access is controlled carefully: each partner sees only the information meant for them.

Remember

  • The Internet is a global network of networks using TCP/IP; the Web is one service on it.
  • An intranet is private; an extranet gives selected outsiders controlled access.

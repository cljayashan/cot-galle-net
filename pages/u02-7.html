2.7Cabling

Twisted pair Ethernet cables are wired to either the T568A or T568B standard.

  • Straight-through cable: same standard at both ends. Connects unlike devices (PC to switch).
  • Crossover cable: T568A at one end and T568B at the other. Connects like devices (PC to PC, switch to switch), though most modern devices use Auto-MDIX and do not need it.

Maximum UTP segment length is 100 metres.

Why there are two standards

Both standards use the same eight pins and the same RJ45 connector. The only difference is that the orange and green pairs swap places. Either standard works, but a site should pick one (T568B is the most common in commercial cabling) and use it everywhere, so that every cable is wired the same way.

Why crossover cables existed

In 10 and 100 Mbps Ethernet, a PC sends on pins 1 and 2 and receives on pins 3 and 6. A switch does the opposite. With a straight-through cable between a PC and a switch, the PC's "send" pins meet the switch's "receive" pins, which is correct. Two PCs connected straight-through would both send on 1 and 2 and nobody would hear anything, so a crossover cable swaps the pairs. Gigabit Ethernet uses all four pairs in both directions, and almost all modern ports have Auto-MDIX, which detects the cable type and adjusts automatically.

There is also a rollover (console) cable, which is used to connect a PC's serial or USB port to a switch or router's console port for first-time setup (see 3.2.1). It is not used for network traffic.

Making a cable: step by step

  1. Cut the cable to length and strip about 2 to 3 cm of the outer jacket, without cutting the inner wires.
  2. Untwist the pairs and arrange them in the T568B order (or T568A).
  3. Flatten and straighten the wires, then trim them evenly to about 1.2 cm.
  4. Push them into the RJ45 plug with the clip facing away from you, until every wire reaches the end of the plug. The jacket should go inside the plug so that it is gripped.
  5. Crimp firmly with the crimping tool.
  6. Test with a cable tester: all eight lights should come on in order.

The 100 m rule

A UTP link from the switch to the device must not be longer than 100 metres: normally up to 90 m of fixed cable in the walls plus up to 10 m of patch cords at the two ends. Longer runs lose too much signal. For longer distances, use fibre or put a switch in between.

Common mistakes: untwisting the pairs too far (more than about 1 cm) causes crosstalk; the jacket not reaching inside the plug means the wires pull out; mixing T568A and T568B by accident creates a crossover cable.

Remember

  • Same standard at both ends gives a straight-through cable; different standards give a crossover.
  • Modern ports use Auto-MDIX, so a straight-through cable works almost everywhere.
  • A UTP link must not be longer than 100 m.

Unit 7 · Domain Name System (DNS)

7.6Obtaining and registering a domain

Who is involved: ICANN coordinates the namespace; a registry runs each TLD (for example Verisign for .com; LK Domain Registry for .lk); a registrar is the company you buy from; the registrant is you.

Steps to register:

  1. Choose a name: short, memorable, relevant, easy to spell; check trademarks.
  2. Check availability with the registrar's search or a whois lookup.
  3. Select the TLD (.com, .org, .lk and so on; some zones have eligibility rules and require documents, for example .ac.lk for academic institutions).
  4. Pick an accredited registrar and compare price, renewal cost and support.
  5. Provide registrant details (name, address, email, phone) and pay; registration is for 1 to 10 years.
  6. Enable protections: WHOIS privacy, auto-renew, registrar lock and two-factor authentication.
  7. Set name servers: use the registrar's DNS, a hosting provider's, or your own (create glue records if your name servers are inside the domain, for example ns1.example.com).
  8. Create records: A/AAAA for the website, MX for email, TXT for SPF/DKIM and verification.
  9. Wait for propagation: changes may take minutes to 48 hours depending on TTL; verify with dig or an online checker.
  10. Renew on time or the domain may expire and be lost.

Subdomains (for example portal.example.com) are free to create once you own the domain.
